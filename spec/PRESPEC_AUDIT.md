# Pre-specification: audit-budget study (frozen before any run)
Date: 2026-11-26. Context: TPC (PRESPEC_TPC.md) failed HT1 and HT2; reported as a negative result, not retuned.

## Question
Under one-sided label poisoning, how many audited (clean-label) negatives are needed to restore a fixed-FPR threshold, and does the error follow a predictable law?

## Rules (all added to the frozen set; nothing existing is changed)
aud_f: empirical (1-alpha) quantile of the scores of audited true negatives, audit fraction f of the validation window, f in {0.2%, 0.5%, 1%, 2%, 5%}; fallback to the naive rule if fewer than 5 audited negatives.
audnp_f: Neyman-Pearson umbrella order statistic (delta = 0.05) on the same audited negatives (same fallback).
trust (f = 5%, existing) is kept as a cross-check.

## Design
Noise: random, delay, delay2; poison 0, 0.2, 0.4; operating points FPR 1% and 0.1%; learners LR/RF/XGB; strategies BASE, CW.
Development sets: creditcard, IEEE-CIS, UNSW-NB15. Confirmation sets (run unchanged, analysed after dev): Elliptic, CIC-IDS2017.

## Hypotheses (Holm over all tests within a hypothesis, alpha 0.05)
HA1: aud_f with f >= 1% lowers absolute calibration |log2(FPR/target)| versus naive on poison >= 0.2, significantly in a majority of dataset x noise x operating-point cells on the development sets, and in at least one confirmation set.
HA2 (audit-budget law): with n_a = number of audited negatives and alpha the target FPR, the expected exceedances n_a * alpha determine the error. Prediction: log mean abs calibration decreases linearly in log(n_a * alpha) with slope in [-0.6, -0.4] (95% bootstrap CI over windows, units with n_a * alpha >= 1, abs calibration floor 1e-4 as before). Reported separately for each noise type.
HA3: the audnp_f rule keeps FPR <= alpha in at least 90% of test windows when noise is random (same-distribution case); under delay/delay2 any violation rate is reported, not hypothesised.
Failure of any hypothesis is reported as such. No threshold, grid, fraction or exclusion rule is changed after seeing results.

## Honest novelty note
NP-umbrella thresholds and audited subsets are established tools (Tong et al. 2018; Yao et al. 2021). The claimed contribution is the quantified audit-budget law under one-sided poisoning and its failure boundaries, not the thresholds themselves.
